# Assessments

Answer without running code first, then check your reasoning. Teachers can use the answer guide after collection. Suggested 2 points per question: 1 for a correct idea, 1 for explanation or calculation.

## Quiz A: weeks 1–4

1. Give a feature and label for predicting whether a bus is late. Why might names be inappropriate?
2. Compute [2, 3] · [0.5, −1].
3. What is the derivative of x² at x=3? What does it describe?
4. Why must a test set be kept separate from model selection?
5. A dataset has 90 negatives and 10 positives. What accuracy does an always-negative classifier achieve? What is its positive recall?

## Quiz B: weeks 5–10

1. Given TP=8, FP=2 and FN=4, calculate precision and recall.
2. What does gradient descent do, and what might happen if the learning rate is too large?
3. Why is a nonlinear activation useful in a neural network?
4. What is an embedding? Why must the same tokenizer mapping be used to encode and decode?
5. What does a causal attention mask hide, and why?

## Assignment rubric (10 points each)

- Correctness and complete calculations: 4
- Experiment and evidence, including reproducible code when relevant: 3
- Explanation and limitations: 2
- Clarity and attribution: 1

## Capstone rubric (100 points)

- Working, reproducible model and generation: 25
- Data provenance, split and leakage discussion: 15
- Baseline, held-out measurement and comparison: 20
- Controlled experiment and interpretation: 15
- Model card, responsible use and limitations: 15
- Clear presentation and answers: 10

The model is graded on sound reasoning and evidence, not how human its output sounds. See [capstone](../capstone/README.md).
