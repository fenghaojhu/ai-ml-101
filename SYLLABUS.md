# Syllabus

**Audience:** high-school students comfortable with variables, loops, functions and Python. Algebra is helpful; calculus concepts are introduced intuitively. Work individually or in pairs. Suggested rhythm: 45 minutes reading, 60–90 minutes lab, 60 minutes assignment, and discussion/reflection each week. Modules spanning two weeks use the extra time for practice.

By the end you can frame a prediction problem, prepare a leakage-free evaluation, compare a model with a baseline, explain gradients and attention, train a tiny language model and discuss limitations and impacts.

## Weekly path

1. AI tasks, data and consequences; 2. vectors and functions; 3. derivatives and probability; 4. splits and baselines; 5. regression and gradient descent; 6. classification and metrics; 7. neural networks; 8. tokens and embeddings; 9. attention; 10. causal transformers and capstone proposal; 11. train and evaluate; 12. experiment, model card and presentation.

Suggested grading: assignments 35%, quizzes 20%, capstone 35%, participation and reflection 10%. Teachers may adapt these weights. The [assessment guide](assessments/README.md) includes quiz questions and a rubric. Explain reasoning and document assistance; do not submit generated text or code you cannot explain.
